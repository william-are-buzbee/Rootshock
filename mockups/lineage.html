Lowfield Lineage

One tissue, grafted from body to body. Five testers took it through the products, Holt through a burn. The crop is generation 1; the elders are 2. Every mutant on the station descends from one of them, and looks like it. Every green form is wild tobacco, Nicotiana rustica, mutated its own way (not every line shows it). Almost nothing on the station still looks human. The staff were as racially mixed as the country, and a mutant keeps its host's skin.

The testers lived in a large isolation room in Horticulture, a small village built to hold six people for as long as a trial ran. They were encouraged to repeat the same dose, for consistent reports. Nobody expected worse than a bad night.

Mary Holt

Took
Raw tissue from the beds, through a burn on her hand.
Way round
Care. She grafts the plants onto herself slowly and on purpose, choosing each one, and has never stopped.
Now
The green god, and a level. Horticulture is colonised by her; the cave under it is her body entire, floor, cane, roof and walls, one plant on one root. Her face is in it somewhere, a person's size, eyes closed: a dot in an ocean. Massive sunflowers lean among her, too heavy to hold their heads up to the light, ringed by small ones; Pia's tobacco flowers come out of the rock and the floor.
Makes
The crop, pushed the way a farmer pushed wild corn: leaves big enough to roof a room, stalks strong enough to build with.
Children
Her cultivars, bred, not born. In the cave, her hunters: one generation of plant-like predators grafted from parts of her, the least human things on the station.
Open
Her tell, and how she can be hurt.

Inside Every other elder can only make more of itself; Holt can make something new. The only one who plans by growing: Tom is her longest bet, and Sam carries her light. Her root system is the station's one true hivemind; she feels footsteps on soil, not on stone.

Inside The opiate dulls with each generation; the fear does not. Pia's fear of eradication was a thought; in the founder it became biology, and the Commons have carried it ever since. Pia infected nobody on purpose. The founder did. So the youngest are the most violent, the oldest the largest and most stupefied. What the Commons carry is the early snapshot of her, the flesh and the opiate, not the tree. Grafting her takes the edge off, and you lose something you don't miss. Old Pias grow faster as they age, so the tree is a clock.

Dale Mercer

strong leaf · employee of the monthheadless
Crisis
Weeks without sleep: rage, blackouts. The energetics were born then, and he barely remembers them.
Way round
His brain learns to ignore the stimulant. He sleeps, and he is himself again. The organ never stops making it.
Now
No head at all: a nicotine-stained chest on two knotted, too-long legs, sat in a tub of his own stimulant, one knee up and one leg over the side. Tobacco leaf grows out of him in shelves, like bracket fungus, the colour of nicotine. One hole low in his front leaks into the tub. No throne; the tub's high back only reads as one.
Spreads by
Low doses left wherever people drink, and grafts from his eldest for rapid immunity. He won't graft anyone himself.
Children
His mutations run to size. His eldest look like him: chests on legs, the same colour, fewer leaves, less ooze, each wrong its own way; about half keep a head, tobacco-coloured, leaf out of the top; the small have a navel where the big have the hole. The growth goes into their legs, as his did: one with a hole stands at least a third taller than one without. The sleepless, the same kind still in it, and mad. The antidote child. Late children, mild.
Tell
The sleepless grind their teeth (you hear it), don't blink, never stop moving. If it sits down, it's past it.

Inside He is inoculating the station. There are too many sleepless children for his death to fix anything, so he is trying to get ahead of them. Nobody wants a second dose: it means a bad night. A graft from his eldest costs a few nights awake on the edge, then you are immune, your blood carries it, and the sleepless keep away from your smell. The antidote child breaks the drug down instead, and wants the spreading stopped, not managed.

A zone of his own, which Holt moved him to and sealed with her roots. Round him, the smaller producers he threw, grown up out of the floor.

A graft like any other: they take in a wound, or after long breathing of them, not in one breath. The rebreather keeps them out.

Inside Holt isn't containing him; she is saving him for the surface. Opening his zone lets him out on your schedule instead of hers. The gentlest elder is the one most likely to end the world.

Sam Okoro

a bit of everything≈ 3.3 m
Took
Every product at least once, and the niche strains nobody else touched: a DMT-like, a psilocybin-like. The trial's most watched subject.
Way round
None needed. Moody like Janus, kind like the aedra: grumpy and dim on a bad day, full of love and energy on a good one. Never psychotic, never violent. Driven to fairness.
Now
A big man, about 3.3 m, long in the leg, a patchwork of pieces swapped with friends, still in a loincloth: what he keeps of being a man. The whites of his eyes are neon, the pupils black. His left arm glows, brighter than the mushrooms crowded on that side of him.
Spreads by
Gifts. A graft is how he shows affection.
His graft
Treats the mind, not the body: sleeplessness still wrecks you, but you stop being violent about it, and can carry on. The soundtrack gains something that isn't in the room.
Tell
He is a light. His blood glows the arm's colour, but only when it is out of him.

Inside The arm's light was Holt's gift, and it does two more things. Her heliotropes follow light, so they always know where he is. And the spores in his blood are her tissue: they don't let her read him, but they make him fond of her. His love for the universe is his own; his warmth toward Holt was grown.

The young gods

The founder. Touched by Pia in her stupor. He carried the plants' fear as his own terror, took others by force, and turned the Commons. Where Pia's daze becomes violence.

The mother. A woman of the Commons bloc whose graft took in the womb. Touch her and you lay eggs too. Her hatchlings have no host, and lose most of the human look.

Security's first. Taken by a hatchling in Cargo, so it inherited no fear and no thought, only drills. It sits up out of the middle of the overseer. Security tends the nest it can't add to, and wants a mother of its own.

The rules

  1. Strain, rootstock, habit. You inherit your grafter as they were on the day, on your own body, and your body grows whatever you do.
  2. Time alone makes a strain. Change without passing it on and you drift; change beside someone and you drift together.
  3. Every organ makes a problem, and the body grows a way round it. The crisis is the time that takes, and it leaves scars the solution keeps.
  4. Young tissue carries the drug; old tissue carries the cure. The same graft cures the sick and infects the clean.
  5. You age into your solution. Stillness goes to plant, action to flesh. Elders are big, each in its own way; an elder who isn't big has a reason.
  6. An idea in the parent is an instinct in the child. Effects dull with each generation; instincts don't.
  7. Mutation keeps what the human had. Mutants still sleep, so any of them can be kept awake: Dale's tub is a danger to all of them.
  8. The mutagen keeps you changing, not alive. It can't raise the dead or stop a bleed, and it slows without new grafts.
